Single sign-on (SSO) lets people sign in to June through Okta instead of a June password or Google. Once it is on, everyone whose email is on one of your domains must use it.

SSO is for signing in. To have Okta also add and remove people and set their roles, add [SCIM](/admin/scim) after SSO works.

## Requirements

| Requirement | Detail |
|---|---|
| Plan | Enterprise |
| Who can set it up | Owner or Admin |
| Email domain | At least one, added under **Settings > Email Domains** |
| Identity provider | Okta, using OpenID Connect (OIDC). One provider per organization |
| Okta access | You can create app integrations and edit an authorization server |

This is the Okta sign-in app. It is separate from the [Okta integration](/integrations/okta), which syncs your employees and applications into June.

## Set up Okta SSO

<Steps>
  <Step title="Add your email domain in June">
    Go to **Settings > Email Domains** and add the domain your people sign in with. Until you do, the Okta card on **Settings > Authentication** is disabled and shows "Email domain required".
  </Step>
  <Step title="Create the app in Okta">
    In the Okta admin console, go to **Applications > Applications**, click **Create App Integration**, choose **OIDC - OpenID Connect**, then **Web Application**, and click **Next**. Name it, for example, `June SSO`.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Set the redirect URI and assignments">
    Set **Sign-in redirect URI** to `https://auth.juneops.com/oauth2/idpresponse`. Under **Assignments**, choose who can use the app: everyone, selected groups, or skip for now. Assigned people can sign in to June through Okta. If [SCIM](/admin/scim) is off, they join June as a Member the first time they do. With SCIM on, the SCIM app adds people instead. Click **Save**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Copy the client credentials">
    On the app's **General** tab, under **Client Credentials**, copy the **Client ID** and **Client Secret**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Add an access rule to your authorization server">
    Go to **Security > API > Authorization Servers** and open the default server or a custom one. Under **Access Policies**, add a policy, then **Add Rule** with the **Authorization Code** grant type. June requests the `openid`, `profile` and `email` scopes.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Copy the Issuer URI">
    On the authorization server's page, copy the **Issuer URI** exactly as shown, including its full path. It must be an okta.com address, such as `https://TENANT.okta.com/oauth2/default`. Custom Okta domains are not accepted.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Enter the details in June">
    Go to **Settings > Authentication** and click the **Okta** card.

    <Frame caption="Settings > Authentication, with the Okta card">
      <img src="/images/admin/sso-providers.png" alt="The Authentication page with the heading Available SSO Providers and an Okta card" />
    </Frame>

    Enter **Issuer URL**, **Client ID** and **Client Secret**, then click **Save**.

    <Frame caption="The Okta configuration form">
      <img src="/images/admin/sso-okta-form.png" alt="The Okta SSO form with Issuer URL, Client ID and Client Secret fields and Cancel and Save buttons" />
    </Frame>
  </Step>
  <Step title="Confirm">
    <Warning>
      Once SSO is on, everyone on your email domains, including you, signs in through Okta. If a mistake in the Okta app locks people out, contact support@juneops.com.
    </Warning>

    June shows "Configure SSO Provider" with the message "All existing users will need to sign back in for SSO authentication to take effect." That means each person's next sign-in goes through Okta. Nobody is signed out when you click **Configure**.

    The dialog also says setup can take a few minutes, during which June may be briefly unavailable. Choose a quiet time. Click **Configure**. June shows "SSO Provider Configured".
  </Step>
</Steps>

When it works, **Settings > Authentication > Okta** shows a green **Active SSO Provider** box and your configuration, with the secret masked.

## What changes for people

- **Signing in** works from the June sign-in page: enter the work email, click **Continue**, and Okta takes them the rest of the way. If someone tries a password or Google instead, June sends them to Okta, or asks them to continue with their work email.
- **New people** get a June account the first time they sign in, with their name and email from Okta. They join as **Member**. Change their role under **Settings > Members**. See [Members and roles](/admin/roles).
- **Existing accounts** are matched by email, so people keep their history.
- **With SCIM on**, signing in through Okta no longer adds anyone. Okta assigns people.

## Update or rotate credentials

**Who can do this:** Owner, Admin.

1. Go to **Settings > Authentication > Okta** and click **Edit**.
2. Change the fields you need. Leave **Client Secret** blank to keep the current secret.
3. Click **Update**, confirm **Update SSO Provider**, and June shows "SSO Provider Updated".

## Remove SSO

**Who can do this:** Owner, Admin.

Click **Delete** on **Settings > Authentication > Okta**, then confirm. People keep their access to June and sign in with email and password or Google again. If SCIM was on, it turns off too.

## If something looks wrong

| What you see | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| The Okta card is disabled | No email domain yet | Add one under **Settings > Email Domains** |
| "Invalid Okta Issuer URL" | The Issuer URL is not an okta.com address in the form `https://TENANT.okta.com` | Copy the Issuer URI again from your authorization server. It must be on okta.com, because custom Okta domains are not supported |
| "Client Secret is required when configuring a new SSO provider." | The secret field is empty on first setup | Paste the Client Secret from Okta |
| Sign-in fails right after you save | June is still applying SSO, which can take a few minutes | Wait and try again |
| "Your organization requires SSO. Please continue with your work email." | The person tried a password or Google on your domain | Enter the work email and continue to Okta |
| "SSO is disabled for your organization. Please use another method to sign in." | SSO was removed | Sign in with email and password or Google |
| "Your account is inactive. Please contact your administrator." | The person's account is deactivated | Ask your Owner or an Admin |
| "Cannot remove email domain" | An organization always needs at least one email domain | Add the new domain first, then remove the old one |
