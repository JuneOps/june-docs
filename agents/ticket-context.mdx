title: 'Ticket context'
description: 'See who asked and the details IT needs about the person a ticket is for, as an internal note on the Jira ticket, and see tickets linked to each person and device.'
---

When a ticket arrives in the Jira project June tracks, June adds an internal note saying who asked, with their name, title and department. For common kinds of request, the note also has the details IT needs about the person the ticket is for.

The kinds are onboarding and offboarding, device problems, sign-in problems, AI usage (such as a request for a higher limit) and software access. For example, an onboarding note shows the new hire's start date, their manager and whether their accounts exist yet. A device note shows the person's devices and when each last checked in.

Announcements and FYI tickets get no note. Other requests get only who asked.

This is separate from [Context and memory](/agents/context-and-memory), which is the knowledge June uses in chats.

## Who the ticket is for

June looks for a person named in the ticket title, @-mentioned, or whose email address is in the ticket. AI then decides whether the ticket is actually for them. Someone tagged only to stay informed, an approver, or a colleague named in "same access as" is not.

When June is less sure, the person's heading says "please confirm". When June cannot tell, the note says "June couldn't tell who this ticket is for" and shows no one's details. June never shows the requester's details in place of someone else's.

## Who sees it, and when

The note is internal, so requesters don't see it in the portal. June adds notes only in Jira Service Management projects. In other project types, June links tickets to people but adds no note.

The note is a snapshot of when it was written, and Jira shows when. If June later works out who the ticket is for, or decides it is about someone else, it adds one short follow-up note. It never edits earlier notes, and it adds nothing to closed tickets or to tickets created before ticket context was turned on.

## Tickets on employee and device pages

June links each ticket to the people it is about, so it shows on the **Tickets** tab of their employee page. For device tickets, it also shows on the pages of their devices. Click a row to open the ticket in Jira.

**Who can see the tab:** Member, Admin and Owner (permission **Requests & linked tickets**). Software Procurement and AI Usage don't have it.

## Turn it on or off

**Who can do this:** Admin and Owner, who can set up integrations.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Connect Jira and register the webhook">
    Follow [Jira](/integrations/jira) to connect Jira, then [register the webhook](/integrations/jira#webhook-configuration) and save its secret. The webhook's JQL filter must include your IT project.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Enter the project key">
    Under **Settings** > **Integrations** > **Jira**, enter your IT project's key in **Default Project Key**, for example `IT`. June tracks one project, so enter one key.
  </Step>
</Steps>

**Automatically enrich tickets**, in the **Webhooks** section of the Jira integration page, is on by default. Turn it off to stop the notes and the links. Links already made stay in June.

June adds these notes on its own; see [When June acts without asking](/agents/agent#when-june-acts-without-asking).

## If something looks wrong

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="June didn't add a note">
    Check that the ticket is in the project entered in **Default Project Key**, that the Jira connection is active, and that the webhook's JQL filter includes the project. Announcements, closed tickets and tickets created before ticket context was turned on get no note. Projects that aren't Jira Service Management get none either.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="The note is about the wrong person, or no one">
    Put the person's full name in the ticket title, @-mention them, or include their exact email address. June matches the reporter to an employee by email address, then by full name, so make the employee's details in your directory match their Jira account. If a Jira account hides its email address, make it visible in the person's Atlassian profile or give the account the person's full name. See [Employees](/people/employees). A missing person is safer than a wrong one, so June leaves the details out when it isn't sure.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
