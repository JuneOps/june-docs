The Agent is June's assistant. You ask in plain language, in the web app or in Slack, and June looks things up, answers with what it found, and takes action in your connected systems. Employees use it in Slack and Jira to get their own IT help, and June hands the work to IT when it can't finish.

## How the pieces fit

- [Agent](/agents/agent): where you and your employees talk to June, and where approvals happen.
- [Tools](/agents/tools): the actions June can take, and which roles can use each one.
- [Context and memory](/agents/context-and-memory): what June knows about your company: rules you write, and facts June saves.
- [Runbooks](/agents/runbooks): end-to-end workflows June runs on its own, on demand, on a schedule, or when a request or ticket arrives.
- [Access policies](/agents/access-policies): the rules for who can get an app, who approves, and how access is granted and removed.
- [Ticket context](/agents/ticket-context): a comment on your Jira tickets with the reporter's details and devices. Separate from Context and memory.

### Set up June's agent

1. Connect the integrations June will act in, such as [Okta](/integrations/okta), [Google Workspace](/integrations/google-workspace), [Jamf Pro](/integrations/jamf-pro) or [Slack](/integrations/slack).
2. Check which roles can use which tools, under **Settings** > **Roles**.
3. Write your [context](/agents/context-and-memory).
4. Turn on Slack for your team and your employees (see [Employees in Slack and Jira](#employees-in-slack-and-jira)).
5. Add [access policies](/agents/access-policies) for the apps employees ask for.
6. Build [runbooks](/agents/runbooks) for work you repeat.

## When June acts without asking

| Where | What happens |
|---|---|
| Looking things up | Read tools never need approval. June answers, builds reports, and waits for employee replies on its own. |
| Chats | Actions in your connected systems always need approval. June shows each one on a card and runs it only after an approver chooses **Approve**. A runbook you start from a chat follows the Runbooks row. When you ask in a Personal chat, June publishes or deactivates a custom tool without a card. |
| Runbooks | Runbooks are end-to-end workflows that run on their own. Each action you add runs without approval unless you require it, apart from the cases below. |
| Employee Request runbooks | Approval for an action is an approval chain, such as the requester's manager. It is off until you add one. |
| Access policies | A policy with no approval step approves on its own; one with approvers waits for them. June then grants and later removes access itself through an Okta or Google group or a custom tool, or asks IT to do it with a **Manual task** (the default). See [Access requests](/help-desk/access-requests). |
| AI limit policies | A tier with no approval step raises the limit on its own; one with approvers waits for them. June then changes the limit in Claude or ChatGPT itself. See [AI limit requests](/help-desk/ai-limit-requests). |
| Notes and notifications | June writes to Jira and Slack on its own, for example: the [Reporter Details comment](/agents/ticket-context), a Jira tracking ticket (kept in sync with the request) and a Slack notifications thread when it hands a request to IT, access notices to requesters and IT, and runbook failure alerts. |
| Memory | June saves durable, organization-wide facts without asking. It never saves private or person-specific details. |

Runbook actions still ask for approval when:

- June would use a value other than one the author fixed.
- June would run a write action that isn't placed.
- June would send a reachout (a message that collects replies) to more than 25 people or a changing audience.
- June would send a reachout that starts an action from the replies, or that personalizes messages from a data query.

A typed "yes" in conversation is never approval. Approval comes only from the card.

## Start a chat

**Who can do this:** any role with **Use Agent**: Member, Software Procurement, Admin and Owner. The AI Usage role does not have it.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the Agent">
    Click **Agent** in the sidebar, or the sparkle button in the header from any page. On an asset, employee or software page, June answers in the context of the record you are viewing.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Ask">
    Type your question and press Enter. You can attach a file, such as a PDF or a CSV.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Follow the work">
    The panel on the right shows June's plan and progress as it works. Click **Stop** to end the task.
  </Step>
</Steps>

In a Personal chat, turn on **Knowledge Base** to let June search your company documents. June cites what it used. Owners, Admins and Members can use it. See [Knowledge Base](/help-desk/knowledge-base).

If you paste something that looks like a password or token, June blocks the message and offers to save it as a credential. Saved credentials appear under **Settings** > **Agent** > **Credentials**. See [Tools](/agents/tools#credentials).

## Personal and Shared chats

The left rail has a **Personal** and **Shared** toggle.

| | Personal | Shared |
|---|---|---|
| Who sees it | Only you | Everyone who holds the role you chose, plus anyone with **Access all Shared chats** (Admin and Owner) |
| Role choice | None | Pick one before the first message. It can't change afterwards |
| What June can access | What you can access | What the chosen role can access |

With only one eligible role, June picks it for you.

## Approvals and questions

When June wants to change something, a card appears above the composer and the composer stays locked until you decide.

- Approve or reject each action, or all of them at once.
- Approved actions run independently. If one fails, the ones that succeeded stay done. Rejected actions don't run.
- When June needs an answer instead, a card offers choices or lets you write one.
- In Slack, the same cards appear with **Approve** and **Reject** buttons, and the closed card shows who decided.

**Who can approve:** a role with **Approve Agent & Runbook tool actions** (Admin and Owner by default) that also has permission for that exact action. Without both you see "You don't have permission to approve this action". To let another role approve, add the permission to a custom role in [Members and roles](/admin/roles). Anyone who can use the Agent can answer a question in their own chat.

## Find your chats

Filter task history by state to find tasks that are running, need attention or failed. The Agent item in the sidebar shows a dot when a task is waiting for input or approval.

- **Managed by automation**: a runbook started the chat.
- **Managed in Slack**: the chat lives in Slack; reply from June with **Reply to Slack**.

## Employees in Slack and Jira

Employees don't need a June login.

- **Slack.** Turn it on under **Settings** > **Integrations** > **Slack** > **Slack Settings**. Everything starts off.
  - **June users** and **Employees** each have **Direct messages** and **Channels**.
  - For employees in channels, pick the channels or **Any channel June is in**, then invite June to the channel.
  - In a channel, June answers only messages that tag it.
  - A June user with no employee record is told to use the Agent in June.
  - See [Slack](/integrations/slack).
- **Jira.** Assigning a ticket to June starts a request. See [Jira](/integrations/jira).
- **Microsoft Teams.** Teams doesn't take employee requests or show approval cards. Use Slack.

June answers an employee's own questions, runs a runbook it has for the problem, and requests software access and more AI usage for them, working only on their own data. It never asks for passwords or recovery codes. In a shared channel, it moves the private part to a DM, and secrets go only to the person who asked. When it can't finish, it hands the request to IT; see [Requests](/help-desk/requests).

## If something looks wrong

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="June doesn't answer in a Slack channel">
    June answers only messages that tag it. Check that the channel is selected under **Slack Settings** (or **Any channel June is in** for employees), and that June is in the channel.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="A chat says it is read-only">
    You can read it but not send messages. Slack chats are read-only in June unless you use **Reply to Slack**, and autonomous tasks can be steered only through their decision cards.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="I changed a role but a chat still behaves the old way">
    Removing someone's access takes effect even in open chats. Newly added access reaches new chats only.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
