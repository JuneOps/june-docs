A request is one employee's IT issue, kept as a single timeline: what they asked, what June answered, what IT replied, and IT's internal notes. Open **Requests** in the sidebar to see what needs you, reply, take over from June, or hand a request back.

## Help desk at a glance

- [Ticket context](/agents/ticket-context): on each new Jira ticket, a comment with the details IT needs about the people and devices involved, and links to the people and devices the ticket is about.
- [Requests](/help-desk/requests): every employee IT issue from Slack or Jira, from June's first answer to IT's resolution.
- [Access requests](/help-desk/access-requests): an employee asks for app access, and June runs it through approval, grant and removal.
- [AI limit requests](/help-desk/ai-limit-requests): an employee asks for a higher monthly Claude or ChatGPT limit, and approvers or IT decide.
- [Knowledge Base](/help-desk/knowledge-base): connect Confluence or Google shared drives so June answers questions from your documents.

Requests needs [Slack](/integrations/slack) connected. Connect [Jira](/integrations/jira) with a default project to get a tracking ticket for each request June hands to IT. Employees reach Requests through Slack or Jira. Microsoft Teams conversations don't create requests.

## How requests arrive

Employees never create requests in the June web app. A request starts when:

- **An employee messages June in Slack.** They DM June or tag @June in a channel June is in. One Slack thread is one request. If the employee asks about something else, June suggests starting a new chat.
- **A Jira ticket is assigned to June.** If the reporter doesn't match an employee in June, June adds a comment on the ticket saying it couldn't start the request (internal in Jira Service Management projects; visible to anyone who can open the ticket elsewhere). See [Ticket context](/agents/ticket-context).

## What June does and what needs IT

**June, on its own**

- Answers from your [runbooks](/agents/runbooks), your [Knowledge Base](/help-desk/knowledge-base) and its tools, and closes the request as **Done** when it delivers an answer. Under the answer in Slack, the employee can say whether it solved the problem. Yes records that it worked. No reopens the request and June keeps working.
- Hands the request to IT when it can't solve it, and tells the employee "I've passed this to IT with what we covered and what I tried. You can keep replying here." June then opens the Jira tracking ticket, investigates in the background and leaves its findings as internal notes, and opens a "Handed to IT" thread in your Slack notifications channel.
- Reopens a request it closed if the employee writes again in the thread.

**Needs IT:** replies, status changes and hand backs.

**June never:** shows internal notes to the employee in Slack. For Jira, see [Internal notes](#internal-notes).

## Find what needs you

Open **Requests**. The list shows every request, most recent activity first. A dot on **Requests** in the sidebar means IT has a request that is Open or Waiting for IT. Requests June is still handling never light it.

Search by request ID, Jira ticket ID, title, or requester name or email. Open the filter menu next to the search box to narrow the list:

- **Status**: pick one or more statuses. With none picked, you see all of them.
- **Automation**: for closed requests, how much of the work June did. **Full** means June handled it without IT. **Partial** means June and IT each worked on it. **None** means IT handled it without June.
- **Sort by**: last updated, created date, requester or status, in either direction.
- **View**: the list or the board.

To see what June resolved without IT, set Automation to **Full**. Your filters, sort and view are remembered in your browser.

On the board, change a status by dragging a card or from the card's **...** menu.

Each row in the list shows:

| Column | What it shows |
|---|---|
| Status | The request's status. |
| Request | The title, where it came from (a Jira ticket appears as a link) and the request ID. |
| Requester | The employee, with their department and job title. |
| Actor | Who has the request now, **June** or **IT**, and what happened: June handed it to IT, who closed or canceled it, its Jira ticket was closed, or the employee said June's answer didn't solve it. A bolt after **June** on a closed request means June handled it without IT. |
| Updated | When something last happened on the request. |

## Statuses

| Status | What sets it |
|---|---|
| Open | A new request, IT handed it back to June, or the employee wrote again after June closed it. |
| In progress | IT took over, an access request is running, or the employee answered a question from June. |
| Waiting for employee | IT's reply was delivered, or the employee said June's answer didn't help. |
| Waiting for IT | June handed the request to IT, an access or AI limit request needs IT, or its closed Jira ticket was reopened. |
| Done | IT closed it, June delivered its answer, its access request was granted or rejected, or its Jira ticket was resolved. |
| Canceled | IT canceled it, the employee canceled it through June, or its Jira ticket was canceled. |

Changing a status sends the employee no message of its own, with two exceptions. If the request started in a channel thread and your change takes it over, the thread tells the employee IT is taking over. Canceling a request with a waiting AI limit ask tells the employee it was canceled.

While an access request is running, it owns the request's status. The picker is hidden and board cards show a lock. Work it from its **Access** tab. See [Access requests](/help-desk/access-requests).

## Work a request

Open a request to see the **Employee conversation** timeline, the composer, and a **Details** panel with the requester, source, Jira ticket, who is handling it (**With June** or **IT**), the resolution, and whether the employee confirmed.

**Who can do this:** Owners and Admins, and custom roles created from Admin, can reply and change status. Members see requests read-only, with no composer.

A runbook action waiting for your approval appears on the **Approval** tab, the default tab when there is one. See [Runbooks](/agents/runbooks) for approvals.

### Reply to the employee

Use the **Reply** tab, which appears when the request came from Slack or Jira. If the request started in a channel thread, pick **Thread** or **DM**; June opens a DM if there isn't one. The line under the picker says who can see the reply. Click **Send reply**, or press Cmd/Ctrl+Enter.

To reply and close in one step, click **Send & mark done**. A closed request can't take a reply: change its status to reopen it first.

### Take over from June

When you reply to or change the status of a request June is handling, IT takes it over. The status becomes In progress and the timeline records the takeover. Your reply is the message the employee sees. Once it is delivered, the request moves to Waiting for employee.

### Hand back to June

On an IT-owned, active request, click **Hand back to June**, with an optional note. The request returns to Open with June handling it.

If you add a note, the employee sees it with a line inviting them to keep talking to June in the thread or DM. Without a note, nothing is posted.

Hand back is unavailable when the request is closed, has an access or AI limit request attached, has no employee conversation (a Jira-only request), or while June is still investigating for IT.

### Internal notes

Use the **Internal note** tab. The employee never sees notes in Slack. June also posts each note to the thread in your notifications channel, and to the Jira tracking ticket as an internal comment.

Jira hides internal comments from the reporter only in Jira Service Management projects. In other project types, anyone who can open the ticket can read your notes. This includes the reporter of a Jira-started request.

### Ask June

The **Ask June** tab is a private chat with June about this request, visible only to IT. It is read-only on a closed request, and hidden while an access request, AI limit request or approval is attached.

## Jira sync

When a request has a Jira tracking ticket, June moves the ticket's status to match the request. Replies and notes are copied to the ticket as comments, each marked **Posted via June**. What the employee and June said before the handoff is added as one comment you can collapse.

June sets the employee as the reporter of a tracking ticket it creates. This needs the **Modify Reporter** permission in the Jira project. Without it, the ticket shows June's service account as the reporter and names the employee in the description. See [Add Service Account to Projects](/integrations/jira#step-3-add-service-account-to-projects).

| Request status | Jira ticket moves to |
|---|---|
| Open | To Do |
| In progress, Waiting for employee, Waiting for IT | A status in Jira's In Progress category, using a matching "waiting" status when your workflow has one |
| Done | A done status (not Won't Do, Duplicate or Canceled) |
| Canceled | Canceled, Won't Do or Declined |

Closing the ticket in Jira closes the request in June:

| Jira ticket moves to | Request becomes |
|---|---|
| Canceled, Won't Do, Declined, Rejected or Duplicate | Canceled |
| Any other done status, such as Done or Resolved | Done |

A request with an AI limit ask still waiting is always canceled, whichever done status the ticket moves to.

Reopening a closed ticket in Jira reopens the request in June. The request goes to IT as **Waiting for IT**, whoever closed it before. June does not pick the request back up by itself.

Any other status change in Jira changes nothing in June.

A request stays open when June still has work running on it: an access request that has not finished, access that was already granted, or an AI limit increase that is being applied. June adds an internal note to the ticket saying why.

<Note>
  June recognizes a closed ticket by Jira's **Done** status category, not by the status name. Check that every closed and canceled status in your workflow is in the Done category. A status named "Canceled" in the In Progress category does not close the request.
</Note>

### Comments and edits on the ticket

This covers any Jira ticket tied to a request: a ticket assigned to June, and a tracking ticket June created for a request that started elsewhere.

**Comments**

- **From the requester, while June has the request.** When the request started as a Jira ticket, a public comment from the requester continues the conversation. June answers on the ticket.
- **From the requester, while IT has the request.** The comment shows on the request as the employee's message.
- **From anyone else, and internal notes.** These show on the request's timeline with the person's name, marked as commented in Jira. June does not act on them. Only IT can see internal notes.

June recognizes the requester by their linked Jira account or their email.

A comment on a closed request still shows on the request. On a ticket that started in Jira, June replies once that the request is closed. A comment does not reopen a request. Reopening the ticket in Jira does: the request reopens with IT, as described above.

**Title and description**

Editing a ticket's title in Jira updates the request's title in June.

Editing the ticket's description updates the request's description for requests that started as a Jira ticket. For a tracking ticket June created, the request keeps its own description.

## If something looks wrong

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="The status picker is missing">
    Your role can only view requests, or an access request owns the status. Open the **Access** tab and work it there.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="There is no Reply tab">
    The request has no Slack or Jira conversation to reply to. Use an internal note, or change the status.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="I can't cancel a request">
    A request with granted access can't be canceled until the access is revoked.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
